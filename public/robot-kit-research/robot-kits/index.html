The recommended route

A path, not just a purchase

Given his age, and that he already 3D prints, this is the sequence that gets the most mileage.

1

Start: mBot2 or ELEGOO Smart Robot Car

Bridges block coding into real code with actual sensors. Pick mBot2 for the smoother onramp and the support ecosystem, or ELEGOO if budget is the priority and he's ready to jump straight into Arduino syntax.

2

Level up: Arduino Starter Kit

Once he wants to design his own robot instead of following a kit's blueprint. This is where the 3D printing background becomes a real advantage. He can print custom chassis, mounts, and enclosures around the electronics.

Go deep: Raspberry Pi robotics

When Arduino's limits start to show (vision, more complex logic, networking), a Pi-based robot is the next real ceiling, running actual Python on a full computer.

💡

The one thing that actually matters

Look for kits that teach sensors, not just motors. The ability to add cameras and distance sensors is what bridges toy robots into real robotics concepts. It's the difference between "the wheels turn" and "the robot reacts to the world." Avoid anything that's drag and drop only. He's already past that stage.

Quick answers

FAQ

Do I need to buy the "next step" kit right away? +

No. mBot2 or the ELEGOO kit alone is easily months of material for a 12 year old: sensors, obstacle courses, remixed builds. Let him ask for more before you buy more.

What if he wants to join a robotics competition team later? +

I wouldn't buy a specific platform for that now. Most competitive robotics clubs already have their own hardware and standardize on one system, so it makes more sense to join first and see what they actually use before spending on anything.

What if he's still mostly doing drag and drop Scratch? +

mBot2 is still the right pick. It starts in Scratch-style blocks and grows into Python on the same hardware, so there's no need to re-buy anything once he's ready for real code.
