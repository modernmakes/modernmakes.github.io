# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prosumer and enthusiast FDM 3D printing builders — the self-build CoreXY crowd around Voron, RatRig, VzBot, and Hevort, plus serious hobbyists upgrading hardware (hotends, extruders, probes, motion systems, filament).

They arrive mid-project: deciding which hotend to buy, which mod to print, whether a part is compatible with their toolhead. They are technical, opinionated, and allergic to marketing fluff. They already read forums, Discords, and Reddit; they come to Modern Makes for a synthesized, trustworthy take that saves them the digging. Context is usually a desktop browser at the bench or late-night research, occasionally mobile.

## Product Purpose

Modern Makes is a faceless editorial **curation** publication for prosumer FDM 3D printing. It synthesizes community consensus into clear verdicts, specs, ecosystem hubs, guides, and a hardware database — it does **not** conduct hands-on reviews or fabricate test results.

Success looks like: an enthusiast lands on a hardware or ecosystem page, instantly trusts the framing, gets the answer they came for (verdict, spec, compatibility, where to buy), and comes back as their default reference. Revenue is affiliate + sponsorship, earned by being genuinely useful first.

## Brand Personality

Opinionated enthusiast, three words: **knowledgeable, energetic, honest.** Editorial "we" voice — never "I." It reads like a sharp friend who has followed every release and tells you straight what the community thinks and why. Enthusiast energy is the dominant feeling: the reader should leave thinking "this is made by people who love this as much as I do." Confidence without arrogance; momentum without hype.

## Anti-references

- **Affiliate review-farm.** No "Top 10 Best Hotend 2026" listicle slop, no star-rating clutter, no SEO-bait comparison-table soup, no fabricated "we tested" claims. The word "review" is banned for hardware pages — use specs / verdict / where to buy.
- **Hobbyist forum.** No cluttered, amateur, default-Bootstrap or wiki-grey utilitarian look with no point of view. The design must carry editorial authority.
- **Marketplace / store.** Not e-commerce-first. No buy-buttons-everywhere, no product-grid-as-homepage, no transactional-over-editorial framing. Commerce serves the reader, not the reverse.
- Also avoid: corporate-SaaS gradient-mesh heroes, hero-metric templates, and identical icon-card grids.

## Design Principles

1. **Curation, not reviews.** Synthesize community consensus with factual framing. Never fabricate hands-on opinions, test data, or audience metrics ("available on request" beats a made-up number). Honesty is the brand's moat.
2. **Faceless but opinionated.** The "we" voice has a clear point of view and the confidence to say BUY / CONSIDER / SKIP — without ever becoming a personal "I tested."
3. **Editorial leads, commerce follows.** Voice and usefulness come first; affiliate links and ad slots are earned context, never the hero.
4. **Built for the self-build crowd.** Go deep for the CoreXY ecosystem reader. Expert confidence, real compatibility detail, no dumbing-down — depth is the value.
5. **Enthusiast energy in the craft.** Electric Orange, dark-first sections, decisive typography, and motion that feels alive — the design should radiate the same excitement the audience feels about the hobby.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA.** Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 — watch the Electric Orange (`#FF5A1F`) on light and dark surfaces and the muted grey body color on tinted backgrounds. Full keyboard navigation and visible focus states (already in `global.css`). Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for every animation. Semantic structure with a single H1 per page (enforced via `hasPageH1`).
