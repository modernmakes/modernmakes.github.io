PA12 is unfilled nylon: not the rigid, stiff material that PA-CF gives you, but the flexible, resilient one. Where carbon-filled nylon resists deflection, PA12 bends and springs back. That makes it the correct material for living hinges, snap fits, cable management parts, and anything that needs to survive thousands of flex cycles without fatigue cracking.

The self-lubricating surface is a real advantage. Nylon gears run against nylon or metal surfaces with very low friction and no need for added lubrication. If you're building a printer part that moves against another part repeatedly, PA12 is worth the extra setup complexity over PETG.

That complexity is real. Moisture is the central problem: nylon absorbs water from the air faster than almost any other filament and the resulting prints are bubbly, weak, and dimensionally inconsistent. Dry every spool at 70°C for eight hours before printing and print from an active dryer. Enclosure temperature of 45–50°C is required — cold chamber means warping, full stop. Get those two things right and PA12 is a reliable, repeatable material that unlocks part geometries nothing else can handle.
